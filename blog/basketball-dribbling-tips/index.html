Basketball Dribbling Tips: 9 Drills to Improve Your Handle

A tight handle beats a fast defender. Whether you are a beginner or a veteran, these basketball dribbling tips and 9 drills will build the ball control, ambidexterity and composure that let you play with your head up and your confidence high.

Category: Dribbling Published: August 5, 2026 Updated: August 16, 2026 6 min read

Why a Strong Handle Matters

Dribbling is the skill that unlocks everything else. With a reliable handle you can create your own shot, beat pressure and keep the offense moving. Without it, even the best shooters can be shut out by aggressive defense. The good news: dribbling improves fast with the right drills and a few minutes of daily reps.

Master the Basics First

Before flashy moves, get the foundation right. These basics make every drill more effective.

  • Dribble with your fingertips and pads, not your palm.
  • Keep the ball below your waist for control.
  • Keep your head up to see the court, not the ball.
  • Push the ball to the floor with purpose, not a pat.
  • Stay low with a wide base so you can change direction quickly.

9 Drills to Improve Your Handle

Run these 9 drills in order and you will build a complete handle. Do each for 30-45 seconds, then switch hands.

  1. Stationary pound: dribble hard and low with one hand, then the other.
  2. Two-ball pound: dribble both balls at once to lock in coordination.
  3. Alternating pound: bounce the balls alternately for rhythm.
  4. V-dribble: bounce the ball side to side in a V in front of your body.
  5. Crossovers: switch hands in front with a quick, low push.
  6. Behind the back: circle the ball behind you and switch hands.
  7. Between the legs: bounce through your legs from front to back, then reverse.
  8. Figure 8: move the ball in a figure 8 between and around your legs.
  9. Pound and switch: two hard pounds, then a crossover - repeat at game speed.

Do not rush the ball. Control first, speed second. If you lose control, slow down until every rep is clean.

Drill With a Purpose

Random dribbling won't get you far. Add purpose to your reps by combining drills with movement and defense.

  • Dribble the length of the court while changing hands every two steps.
  • Set up cones or chairs and weave through them at speed.
  • Have a friend try to poke the ball away while you protect it.
  • Practice under pressure with short time limits on each drill.

A handle that only works in an empty gym is not a handle. Train the way you play.

Final Tips

  1. Dribble for 10-15 minutes daily instead of one long weekly session.
  2. Challenge your weak hand first - it will improve fastest.
  3. Keep your head up from day one.
  4. Add game-speed reps once the basics feel easy.
  5. Review your footwork - a fast handle needs fast feet.
  6. Stay relaxed; a tense grip slows every move.
  7. Mix in real shooting so your handles lead to buckets.
