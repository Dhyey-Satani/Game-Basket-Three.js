Baseball Batting Drills to Fix Your Swing for Good

A struggling swing usually does not need a complete rebuild. These baseball batting drills to fix your swing help you improve balance, timing, bat path, and contact one simple step at a time.

Category: Baseball Guide Published: August 22, 2026 Updated: August 22, 2026 2 min read

Find the problem before changing your swing

Start by recording five to ten swings from the side and from behind the hitter. Watch for a collapsing back side, a head that pulls away from the ball, a front shoulder that opens too early, or a finish that leaves the bat across your body. Choose one problem to address instead of trying to correct everything at once. A clear goal makes every repetition more useful.

Build a better swing with tee drills

The tee is one of the best tools for learning a repeatable swing because it removes timing pressure. Place the ball at the front of the plate and practice driving it to the middle of the field. This encourages a direct bat path and helps you feel the barrel working through the hitting zone. Complete three sets of eight to ten controlled swings, resetting your stance after every repetition.

Improve timing with front toss and soft toss

Once your contact point feels consistent, add a moving ball. During front toss, have a partner throw gently from a short distance while you focus on tracking the ball from release to contact. Ask for a mix of strikes rather than fast throws. Your goal is to stay relaxed, start your move on time, and hit through the middle instead of guessing at every pitch.

Quality repetitions teach your swing faster than a bucket of rushed swings.

Train contact, direction, and power together

A good swing is not only about hitting the ball hard. It should produce solid contact to different parts of the field. Use a three-zone drill: send outside pitches to the opposite field, middle pitches up the middle, and inside pitches to the pull side. Have a partner call the target before each toss, or use different tee positions. This teaches your eyes, hands, and lower body to work together.

Use a simple weekly practice plan

Practice two or three times each week for 20 to 30 minutes. Start with five dry swings and a few easy tee swings to prepare your body. Spend the next ten minutes on one mechanical focus, such as staying balanced or keeping the front shoulder closed. Finish with front toss or live batting practice so you can transfer the feeling into a realistic setting.
