5 Effective Baseball Batting Drills to Fix Your Swing

Struggling to find the sweet spot consistently? Mastering your mechanics requires more than just extra reps; it requires the right baseball batting drills to fix your swing.

Category: Baseball Guide Published: August 29, 2026 Updated: August 29, 2026 3 min read

Identify your swing flaws first

Before you start swinging wildly in the cage, you must diagnose what is actually wrong. Most hitters struggle with one of three things: poor hand positioning, a collapsing back leg, or a lunging upper body. If you try to fix everything at once, you will likely end up making your swing even more inconsistent.

The best way to identify these flaws is through video analysis. Set up your phone on a tripod and record your swings from two angles: one from directly behind you and one from the side. Compare your movement to professional players to see where your path deviates from a smooth, rotational plane.

The tee work foundation

Many players skip tee work because they think it is too basic, but it is actually the most important tool in your arsenal. The tee allows you to isolate specific movements without the distraction of a moving ball. This is where you build muscle memory that stays with you during a high-pressure game.

Focus on the drill of hitting to different zones. Instead of just swinging hard, place the tee in the upper, middle, and lower parts of the strike zone. This forces your brain to adjust your swing plane and prevents you from becoming a one-dimensional hitter who can only hit balls right down the middle.

Consistency in the cage leads to confidence at the plate. You cannot master a moving pitch until you have mastered the stationary one.

Improve hand speed and contact

If you feel like you are always late on fastballs, your hands might be too slow or your path might be too long. To fix this, incorporate soft toss drills. Have a partner toss balls underhand from a short distance, aiming them toward different areas of the strike zone.

The goal of these drills is to shorten your swing. A long, looping swing is easy to time, but a compact, direct swing is much harder for pitchers to exploit. Focus on keeping your hands close to your body during the initial phase of the swing to ensure a direct path to the ball.

Driving power through hip rotation

Power does not come from your arms; it comes from the ground up. If you are hitting weak ground balls, you are likely failing to rotate your hips effectively. You need to learn how to transfer weight from your back leg to your front leg while maintaining balance.

Try the step-back drill to build explosive rotation. Start in your hitting stance, step your back foot toward the catcher, and then drive forward into your swing. This teaches your body to use its momentum to whip the bat through the zone rather than just pushing with your shoulders.

Putting it all together

Once you have mastered individual drills, you must transition back to live pitching. There is a massive difference between hitting a stationary tee and timing a 85mph fastball. Start with machine work to simulate velocity, then move to live batting practice with teammates or coaches.

Remember that improvement is a marathon, not a sprint. You will have days where your swing feels perfect and days where it feels completely broken. The key is to return to your fundamental baseball batting drills whenever you feel lost. Consistency in your training routine is what separates the varsity players from the benchwarmers.
