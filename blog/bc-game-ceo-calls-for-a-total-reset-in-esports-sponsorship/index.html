BC.Game CEO Calls for a Total Reset in Esports Sponsorship

In a surprising move that has sent ripples through the competitive gaming community, the CEO of BC.Game has called for a radical fresh start for the brand.

Category: Esports News Published: October 7, 2026 Updated: October 7, 2026 3 min read

A radical proposal for brand rebirth

The landscape of esports sponsorship is often defined by massive contracts and long-term stability. However, the recent statements from the CEO of BC.Game have introduced a concept rarely seen in the industry: the total reset. Instead of looking to accumulate more influence or expand existing partnerships through traditional means, the leadership has proposed a complete overhaul of their current standing.

This proposal is not merely about changing a logo or updating a social media profile. It is a fundamental question of how a brand interacts with its community and the competitive scene. By suggesting a complete restart, the CEO is signaling a desire to move away from previous methodologies and establish a new foundation built on different principles.

Taking all the points away

The most striking part of the announcement was the specific demand to take all their points away. In the context of gaming and brand loyalty, points often represent accumulated status, reward tiers, or historical influence. To voluntarily surrender these assets is a move that defies standard corporate logic, where every brand strives to maximize its existing equity.

Take all our points away. We need a fresh start to ensure we are building something that lasts and truly serves the community.

This sentiment suggests that the current accumulation of status may be hindering the brand's ability to connect authentically with fans. By stripping away the old metrics of success, the company hopes to create a vacuum that can be filled with new, more meaningful engagement strategies that resonate with the modern esports spectator.

Why transparency matters in esports

The esports industry has faced significant scrutiny regarding the ethics of its sponsors. As betting platforms and large-scale gaming corporations become more integrated into professional tournaments, the demand for transparency has never been higher. The CEO's call for a reset can be interpreted as an attempt to address these concerns head-on.

When a brand acknowledges that its current path might be flawed, it gains a level of respect that traditional marketing cannot buy. This pivot could set a precedent for how other major players in the gaming space handle brand evolution and community feedback.

The impact on professional teams and fans

For professional esports organizations, sponsorship stability is everything. A sudden shift in how a major sponsor operates can create uncertainty for teams that rely on these partnerships for operational costs. However, if the reset leads to a more stable and respected brand, the long-term benefits for teams could far outweigh the initial confusion.

Fans are also watching closely. The gaming community is notoriously sensitive to perceived inauthenticity. A brand that tries to force its way into the culture often fails, whereas a brand that shows a willingness to listen and change—even at a significant cost—is much more likely to find a loyal following.

Looking toward a new era of gaming

As we move into a more mature phase of the esports industry, the old ways of doing business are being challenged. We are seeing a shift from pure visibility to deep, value-driven engagement. The BC.Game announcement is a symptom of this larger trend where the 'how' of sponsorship is becoming just as important as the 'how much'.

Whether this reset succeeds or fails remains to be seen, but it has certainly sparked a necessary conversation about the future of brand-community relationships. It reminds us that in the fast-paced world of competitive gaming, even the biggest players must remain humble and willing to evolve.
