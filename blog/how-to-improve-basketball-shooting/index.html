How to Improve Your Basketball Shooting: 7 Simple Tips

Good shooters aren't born - they're built one rep at a time. Whether you shoot a few times a week or live at the gym, these 7 simple tips will make every shot more consistent and raise your confidence the moment the ball leaves your hands.

Category: Basketball Shooting Published: July 28, 2026 Updated: August 14, 2026 7 min read

Why Shooting Technique Matters

Scoring in basketball is a mix of skill, repetition and smart decisions. Technique matters because your body repeats what it learns. Shoot with a flawed motion and you will repeat that flaw under pressure - in the final minutes of a close game, when it counts the most.

The good news is that fixing your technique is faster than you think. Small changes to your feet, hands and follow-through can add a surprising number of points to your average, and most of them take just a few minutes of focused practice to feel natural.

Improve Your Stance

Every shot starts from the ground up. A balanced stance gives your body a stable platform and transfers power smoothly from your legs into the shot.

  1. Feet shoulder-width apart with your shooting-side foot slightly forward.
  2. Square your hips toward the rim, or use a slight angle if it feels more natural.
  3. Bend your knees and keep your weight on the balls of your feet.
  4. Keep your head still - rising with a quiet head keeps your aim steady.

If you ever feel off balance at the top of your shot, check your stance first. It is almost always the culprit.

Work on Your Release

The release is where good shooters separate themselves. Your shooting hand should finish high, straight and relaxed, with the ball rolling off your fingertips for a soft backspin.

  • Keep your elbow under the ball rather than flaring out to the side.
  • Release the ball at the top of your jump, not on the way down.
  • Let the ball leave through your index and middle fingers.
  • Hold your follow-through until the ball reaches the rim.

A great drill: shoot one-handed from close range using only your shooting hand. This forces your wrist and elbow to do the work and builds a clean, repeatable release.

Practice Consistently

Consistency beats intensity. Shooting for 15 minutes a day, five days a week will improve your form far more than a three-hour session once a month. Your brain and muscles need regular reinforcement to build muscle memory.

"You miss 100% of the shots you don't take." - Wayne Gretzky

Use focused drills instead of just messing around. Set a target number of makes from a few favorite spots, track your percentage, and slowly extend your range. If you want a fun way to train your timing, jump into Basketball Arena and practice your aim under a timer.

Final Tips

Putting all 7 tips together, here is your simple checklist before every shot:

  1. Set your feet - balanced and squared.
  2. Grip the ball with your shooting hand centered.
  3. Bring the ball up in one smooth motion.
  4. Rise and release at the top of your jump.
  5. Finish with a high follow-through.
  6. Stay relaxed - tension kills accuracy.
  7. Repeat the same motion every single time.

Improving your shooting is a journey, not a switch. Keep practicing, and remember to enjoy the process - every made shot is progress.
